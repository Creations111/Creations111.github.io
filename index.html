Fraction Frenzy educational board game Sheet 1 of 2, April 2026

Conveyor Belt Fraction Board Game

Cornerstone of Engineering II, Northeastern University. Team of five.

A competitive, hands-on game built for 5th graders at Melrose Leadership Academy in Oakland, CA, who were struggling with adding fractions with unlike denominators. Teams solve problems on an LCD and keypad; a correct answer spins an LED wheel, and a motor-driven conveyor belt carries their game piece forward. First to position 12 wins, and the board resets itself.

My roleCAD Lead and Project Manager
I designedAll SolidWorks parts and the full assembly, the enclosure and conveyor belt system, dowel and motor stands, 3D-printed game pieces, belt tensioning
HardwareRaspberry Pi Pico, two DC motors with TB6612FNG driver, two 20×4 LCDs and 4×4 keypads over I2C, 8-LED spinner
SoftwareMicroPython with asyncio so both teams can type answers at the same time
FabricationLaser-cut plywood enclosure, 3D-printed parts, rubber and sandpaper belts
Budget$64 out of pocket for a build worth about $135

Takeaway: belt guides and the spinner should have been planned from day one, and I want to be as comfortable with wiring and code as I am with CAD.

Side proximity detection vehicle safety prototype Sheet 2 of 2, December 2025

Vehicle Side Proximity Detection System

Cornerstone of Engineering I, Northeastern University. Solo project.

Cars usually have front and rear sensors, but the sides are left exposed, which is dangerous for cyclists and pedestrians. I built a scale prototype where two ultrasonic sensors on servo motors sweep each side of a car-shaped enclosure. As something gets closer, a buzzer beeps and an LED flashes faster, and nothing fires beyond the threshold to avoid false alarms.

My roleDesigner, builder and programmer
HardwareRaspberry Pi Pico, two HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors, two servos, buzzer, LED
DesignEnclosure in AutoCAD with friction-fit holes, 3D-printed sensor mounts for the servo arms
ProcessChose the design with a Kepner-Tregoe decision analysis; scrapped an early motion-sensor version because it couldn’t detect stationary objects like curbs
TimelinePrototype in November, fully working showcase on December 3, 2025

Takeaway: iteration is part of the process. Going back to the drawing board led to a better sensor choice.
