My role: Undergraduate Research Assistant

I design and make the stands, sample holders, and electromagnets we use to test our samples. I also take magnetostriction measurements and process FMR data with the graduate researchers.

01Problem and requirements

Our lab measures how sputtered magnetic thin films respond to magnetic fields along different axes. Each sample sits on a PCB that has to be held at a precise, repeatable angle between a magnet’s poles, and some samples couldn’t be tested in the lab’s industrial magnets at all.

RequirementWhy it matters
Repeatable sample angleEasy- and hard-axis measurements are only comparable if every sample returns to the same orientation
Quick PCB swapsMany samples are measured in one session
Locks to the rotation stageThe holder and stage must move as one piece, with no play
Rigid and non-magneticFlex shifts the sample, and magnetic material would distort the field being measured
At least 200 G at the sampleThe minimum field needed for samples tested outside the industrial magnets

02Build, test, revise

  1. 1

    3D-printed stands

    Design
    I 3D-printed my first stands so I could make and change them quickly.
    Problem
    Printed plastic flexes much more than metal, and the sample has to stay exactly in place.
    Change
    Redesign the stands to be waterjet cut from aluminum.
  2. 2

    Waterjet-cut aluminum stands

    Design
    I redesigned the stands to be waterjet-cut from aluminum.
    Why aluminum
    It’s non-magnetic, so it won’t distort the field around the sample, and it’s much stiffer than printed plastic.
  3. 3

    Indexed sample holder

    Design
    A 3D-printed holder accepts the sample PCB with about 0.3 mm of clearance, so it slides in and out easily without play.
    Interface
    Features on the bottom of the holder drop into the rotation stage’s holes, locking the holder and stage together as one piece so every sample returns to the same orientation.
  4. 4

    Custom electromagnets

    Need
    Some samples couldn’t be tested in the lab’s industrial magnets, but still needed at least 200 G.
    Design
    I wound my own electromagnets with 250 turns on each coil. To get a stronger field, I designed for more turns, a better core material, and a smaller pole gap.
    Test
    I measured the field with a gaussmeter probe.
    Result
    Up to 800 G (about 800 Oe in air) at under 5 A, four times the 200 G we needed. With that much extra, we can run at lower current, so the coils heat up less and it’s safer to use.

03Measurement and data work

Anisotropy setupPrepared and mounted 8+ sputtered magnetic thin-film samples along easy and hard axes on a GMW-3470 magnet.
MagnetostrictionMeasured deflection across 5+ film compositions on an optical cantilever rig and produced strain vs. field data for analysis with graduate researchers.
FMR processingProcessed angle-resolved FMR data in MATLAB across 31 bias-field orientations and 30 field steps, mapping resonance behavior for 8 devices on a test wafer.
